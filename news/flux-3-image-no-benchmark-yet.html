FLUX 3 Image: We Tested It With Our Own Brief

A benchmark gives context. Your own brief shows what a model can really do.

This is Benito. Grillz, a platinum mullet and a laugh that needs little explanation. He doesn't exist. We made him with FLUX 3 and brought him to life with Seedance 2.5.

We regularly try out new models. To see what we can make with them, what they are good at and how much work is still needed before an image is usable.

What is FLUX 3?

FLUX 3 comes from Black Forest Labs, the Freiburg team behind the earlier FLUX models. It is trained on images, video and audio. That shared foundation is used for several applications, including FLUX 3 Image and FLUX 3 Video.

The image version has been available since 1 October. You can generate images up to native 4K, add up to ten reference images and use bounding boxes to place elements. Useful when you want to work out a composition or make a targeted change.

Since 15 October there is also Klein: a smaller version with open weights that you can run locally.

What do the benchmarks say?

On 19 October FLUX 3 Image ranks 12th on Artificial Analysis for image generation. Black Forest Labs also publishes its own tests in which Klein scores well against other open models.

That gives some context. For us, it only really starts with our own brief. How well does the model follow your directions? Does a character stay recognisable? Can you keep building on an image without starting over every time?

And what did we think?

For this series we worked in Krea. We made six characters, photographed in the same white studio: Benito, Anita, Debby, Kijano, Danny and Django.

The materials stood out most. Skin with texture, convincing chrome and acrylic that catches the light beautifully. Short words and large type also came out well in our tests.

Small text and text in reflections still need attention. You have to keep selecting and steering. One strong result doesn't mean the next generation will be just as good.

So which model should you choose?

That depends on what you want to make. A portrait, a package and a campaign image all ask for different things. That's why we test models with concrete briefs and our own references.

That way you find out fairly quickly which model fits your visual language and where you lose time on corrections.
